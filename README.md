# Asthma: Inside the Airway

A 17-stage p5.js learning story. It follows susceptibility and triggers through immune and neural signals, airway-wall changes and effects on airflow, then reconnects the routes in a final flowchart. Stage 6 is a dedicated, visual guide to the immune cells: where they develop, how T cells and B cells differ, and how an allergen-specific response can lead to IgE on mast cells.

The visual key explains the colours and moving symbols. The epithelial lining is shown as one cell layer; red slashes mark injury, orange dots in the alarmin stage are signals, and blue strands in the T2-low stage are NETs: neutrophil extracellular traps made from DNA and proteins (gold blobs are mucus). Stage 12 explains how neutrophils release these webs, how they can trap microbes, and how they may contribute to ongoing inflammation in some neutrophilic asthma patterns. Hover over underlined terms, airway layers, cells, vessels, mucus and moving particles to see an animated symbol plus three short sections: **plain meaning**, **source / origin**, and **role in asthma**. Scientific shorthand is expanded into everyday language. If two hit areas overlap, the tooltip can show both. Hover explanations stay compact; click a term to hold a longer explanation while you read, then click elsewhere to close it. Comparison labels explain total airflow and the faster local jet that can form at a narrow point; Stage 13 separates sensory input, the vagal ACh reflex, M3 contraction and β2 relaxation; Stage 17 connects the main routes to shared airway effects.

## Open in Visual Studio Code

1. Extract this folder.
2. In Visual Studio Code, choose **File → Open Folder** and select `asthma_pathophysiology_visual_v2`.
3. If needed, install the **Live Server** extension.
4. Right-click `index.html` and choose **Open with Live Server**.
5. Use the Back and Next buttons, numbered circles, or keyboard arrow keys.

The page loads the stable p5.js 2.3.4 release from jsDelivr, so it needs an internet connection. Keep `index.html` and `sketch.js` together in the same folder. Edit `sketch.js`, save, then refresh the browser.

## Stages

1. The four changes that narrow the airway
2. Small-airway anatomy
3. Genetic and environmental susceptibility
4. Triggers and the epithelial barrier
5. Epithelial alarmins
6. Immune-cell guide: bone-marrow and thymus origins; dendritic, CD4 T, Th2, B, plasma and mast cells; parallel ILC2 route
7. Allergic sensitisation and IgE
8. Allergen re-exposure and mast-cell activation
9. Smooth-muscle bronchoconstriction
10. Oedema and mucus
11. Eosinophilic inflammation
12. T2-low inflammation
13. Neural effects: sensory input → vagal ACh → M3 contraction; β2 stimulation relaxes
14. Airway hyperresponsiveness
15. Airway remodelling
16. Effects of narrowing on breathing
17. Whole-pathway flowchart: triggers, immune routes, parallel signals and shared airway effects

All airway drawings are simplified teaching diagrams and are not to scale. The asthma pathways vary between people; the stages show common mechanisms, not a single sequence that applies to every person.

Pathophysiology content is adapted for learning from the [MSD Manual Professional Edition: Asthma](https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/pulmonary-disorders/asthma-and-related-disorders/asthma) and the [GINA 2026 Strategy Report](https://ginasthma.org/2026-gina-strategy-report/). The animation is a simplified branching map: mechanisms vary and do not form one required sequence for every person.
